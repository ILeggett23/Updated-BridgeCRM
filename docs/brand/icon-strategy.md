# BridgeCRM app-icon strategy

## Decision

BridgeCRM's new app icon is **Open Span**: a single rounded arch whose second
support is interrupted and shifted forward. The arch is the immediate reading.
The break and amber support are the memory cue. Together they express the
product's defining behavior: remembered human context becoming a truthful next
step.

The final artwork was selected from five strategic territories only after each
territory worked in black and white. Color is supportive rather than structural.

## 1. Brand truth

### Product utility

BridgeCRM is a local-first, offline-capable relationship CRM for people who need
to remember conversations, keep useful personal context, plan follow-ups, and
move real prospect and customer relationships through an honest pipeline. Its
core job is not contact storage. Its core job is helping someone arrive at the
right next human action with the context to make that action thoughtful.

### Emotional promise

While using Bridge, a person should feel calm, prepared, and respectfully in
control. After using it, they should feel that an important relationship is less
likely to drift and that the next step is clear without feeling pushy or
transactional.

### Behavioral signature

The signature loop is:

1. Capture what happened.
2. Retain only useful relationship context.
3. Surface the next truthful action.
4. Move the relationship forward when the person actually acts.

The interface reinforces this loop through the global Capture action, "What I
Know," Next Up, follow-up lifecycle, relationship health, and exact pipeline
movement.

### Brand personality

The brand should be:

- Thoughtful.
- Grounded.
- Quietly capable.
- Human.
- Purposeful.

The brand should avoid being:

- Corporate-cold.
- Pushy.
- Gamified.
- Artificially clever.
- Visually busy.

### Brand tension

**Structured but human; forward-moving but calm; private by default but built
for connection.**

### One-sentence essence

> BridgeCRM makes the feeling of **staying thoughtfully connected** visible
> through **remembered context and a truthful next step**.

### Icon purpose statement

The icon should make returning users remember the moment when a conversation
stops being a loose memory and becomes a calm, useful next action. It does not
need to explain contact management, analytics, pipelines, reminders, and local
storage at once.

## 2. Repository visual-language audit

- Product name: BridgeCRM; short name: Bridge.
- Existing promise: "Relationships that move forward."
- Primary audience: independent relationship builders, prospectors, customer
  operators, networkers, and small teams who need a human-scale CRM rather than
  enterprise machinery.
- Canvas: warm paper `#F5F2EC`.
- Surfaces: white and raised cream `#FBFAF7`.
- Primary ink: `#1B1913`.
- Brand teal: `#0E6B5C`; strong teal: `#0A4F44`.
- Supporting signals: amber/info, moss/positive, ember/overdue.
- Typography: Newsreader for editorial hierarchy and Inter Tight for interface
  structure.
- UI grammar: compact, mobile-first, flat, warm, editorial, and deliberately
  restrained.
- Strongest product behavior: Capture turns a remembered conversation into
  context, a follow-up, or a pipeline movement without redefining what actually
  happened.
- Previous production icon: a teal at-sign on a warm rounded square. It was
  coherent with the palette but could be read as email, address, or generic
  communication before it could become a Bridge-specific mnemonic.

## 3. Category audit

The comparison set covered both personal-relationship CRMs and mainstream sales
CRMs. Personal-CRM references included folk, Dex, Clay, Notion-as-CRM, Monica,
and Attio. Enterprise adjacency included Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho, Pipedrive,
Close, and Copper. This is a collision audit, not a style board.

Research sources:

- [folk's 2026 personal-CRM comparison](https://www.folk.app/articles/why-you-need-personal-crm)
  identifies the personal-CRM cluster and its shared contact, interaction, and
  reminder behaviors.
- [Dex on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dex-rolodex-and-personal-crm/id1472132715)
  describes the relationship-memory and keep-in-touch use case closest to
  Bridge.
- [Attio on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/attio/id1511545395)
  represents the modular, startup-oriented CRM end of the category.
- [Zapier's 2026 CRM comparison](https://zapier.com/blog/best-crm-app/)
  establishes the mainstream competitive set, including Salesforce, HubSpot,
  Zoho, Pipedrive, Attio, Close, and Copper.

### Repeated visual conventions

- Saturated blue for trust and enterprise software.
- Orange for automation, energy, or sales activity.
- Green for growth and pipelines.
- Single-letter monograms, especially D, P, N, and product initials.
- Clouds, sprockets, nodes, network constellations, contact silhouettes, link
  chains, chat bubbles, handshakes, arrows, charts, and pipeline stages.
- Centered marks inside a conventional rounded-square tile.
- Symmetric geometric marks whose silhouette becomes interchangeable at 24 px.
- Wordmark-first identities that lose their distinctiveness when reduced to a
  launcher icon.

### Unused territory

Bridge can own a warm editorial palette paired with one asymmetrical structural
gesture: an arch that is deliberately incomplete because its missing support has
moved forward. The geometry is not a typed B, not a node graph, and not a
feature collage. Deep forest and warm paper come directly from the product;
amber is reserved for the forward support, so it behaves as a mnemonic rather
than decoration.

## 4. Concept territories

### Territory 1 — Open Span

- **Metaphor:** A bridge whose second support becomes the next step.
- **Product connection:** Context holds the relationship while the app surfaces
  forward action.
- **Emotional promise:** Calm continuity with visible progress.
- **Dominant silhouette:** A heavy rounded arch with unequal supports.
- **Signature move:** The right support is broken and shifted forward.
- **Negative space:** One generous arch opening plus one diagonal handoff gap.
- **Geometry:** 1024-unit square; broad semicircular crown; 190-unit visual
  stroke; rounded terminals; approximately 90-unit lateral handoff.
- **Color:** Deep forest field, warm-paper span, amber forward support.
- **One color:** Span and support merge into one monochrome system while the gap
  retains the gesture.
- **Memory value:** A participant can redraw it as "an arch with the right leg
  stepped away."
- **Ownability:** The abnormal support, not the generic bridge alone, is the
  protected memory hook.
- **Possible misreading:** A lowercase n followed by a period, a doorway, or a
  bent pin.
- **Collision risk:** Moderate with generic arch marks; low with CRM category
  marks because the interrupted structural support is unusual.
- **Blur survival:** One cream arch and one offset warm support.
- **User story:** "Bridge keeps the relationship held while showing me the next
  place to step."

### Territory 2 — Held Thread

- **Metaphor:** An open thread that holds context without trapping the person.
- **Product connection:** Bridge remembers enough to maintain continuity across
  separate conversations.
- **Emotional promise:** Reassurance and openness.
- **Dominant silhouette:** An open circular loop with one inward clasp.
- **Signature move:** The inner thread turns back toward the opening once.
- **Negative space:** A large circular field interrupted by a single controlled
  inward hook.
- **Geometry:** Circular construction, broad constant stroke, rounded terminals,
  offset inner return.
- **Color:** Forest on paper; no accent required.
- **One color:** Fully functional.
- **Memory value:** "An open loop holding a short thread."
- **Ownability:** The inward clasp distinguishes it from an ordinary C.
- **Possible misreading:** Letter C, loading indicator, or refresh symbol.
- **Collision risk:** Higher around circular productivity and sync icons.
- **Blur survival:** Open ring with one interior weight.
- **User story:** "Bridge holds the thread between one conversation and the
  next."

### Territory 3 — Memory Fold

- **Metaphor:** Useful context folded into an object that can be opened later.
- **Product connection:** "What I Know" and conversation history become usable
  preparation, not an archive for its own sake.
- **Emotional promise:** Preparedness and safekeeping.
- **Dominant silhouette:** A clipped octagonal tile with an internal fold.
- **Signature move:** One triangular reveal interrupts the otherwise stable
  enclosure.
- **Negative space:** A large squared internal cut that opens into the fold.
- **Geometry:** 45-degree outer cuts, rectilinear interior, one triangular hinge.
- **Color:** Forest mark on paper, optional amber hinge.
- **One color:** The fold remains clear as negative space.
- **Memory value:** "A folded record with one corner opening."
- **Ownability:** The fold and enclosure share one geometry rather than combining
  a note and a sparkle.
- **Possible misreading:** Letter C, package, or camera aperture.
- **Collision risk:** Moderate with document, crypto, and storage brands.
- **Blur survival:** Dark octagon with one square opening.
- **User story:** "Bridge folds what I learned into the moment I need it."

### Territory 4 — Second Beat

- **Metaphor:** A conversation creates a second beat: follow-through.
- **Product connection:** Logging is not the finish; the next useful action is.
- **Emotional promise:** Human rhythm rather than sales pressure.
- **Dominant silhouette:** A large open beat paired with a smaller offset beat.
- **Signature move:** The second form lands down and forward rather than
  repeating symmetrically.
- **Negative space:** A stable circular opening inside the first beat and a
  generous gap before the second.
- **Geometry:** One ring and one offset disc on a descending diagonal.
- **Color:** Forest first beat, amber second beat.
- **One color:** Relationship between the two beats remains intact.
- **Memory value:** "A big beat followed by a small one."
- **Ownability:** The unequal two-beat rhythm is more specific than generic
  connected dots.
- **Possible misreading:** Magnifier, eyeglasses, or two people.
- **Collision risk:** Moderate-to-high with social, camera, and accessibility
  icons.
- **Blur survival:** One large ring and one smaller dot.
- **User story:** "Every meaningful conversation deserves a second beat."

### Territory 5 — Relay Cut

- **Metaphor:** Context handed from memory into action.
- **Product connection:** Capture and follow-up are two states of one truthful
  record.
- **Emotional promise:** Clarity and momentum.
- **Dominant silhouette:** Two staggered rounded blocks.
- **Signature move:** A single diagonal negative-space cut marks the handoff.
- **Negative space:** A compact white hinge between the blocks.
- **Geometry:** Equal modular blocks on a descending diagonal with one 45-degree
  cut.
- **Color:** Forest source block, amber destination block, or one color.
- **One color:** The diagonal hinge remains visible.
- **Memory value:** "Two blocks handing off through a white cut."
- **Ownability:** The shared cut is more specific than two disconnected tiles.
- **Possible misreading:** Workflow nodes, app switcher, or chain link.
- **Collision risk:** Moderate with automation and collaboration products.
- **Blur survival:** Two offset blocks and a bright central hinge.
- **User story:** "Bridge carries the important detail from what happened into
  what happens next."

## 6. Refinement and selection

The two strongest territories received three structural variants each:

- Open Span A / Balanced: broad crown, compact step, more architectural.
- Open Span B / Forward: narrower crown, greatest lateral movement.
- Open Span C / Quiet: optically calmer crown, shorter break, strongest small
  size balance.
- Held Thread A / Loop: strongest circular continuity.
- Held Thread B / Clasp: strongest interior interaction.
- Held Thread C / Release: largest opening and friendliest silhouette.

**Open Span C / Quiet won.** It retained the best blur and 24–32 px contour,
had the fewest category collisions, fit the product name without relying on a
literal bridge illustration, and translated cleanly to monochrome and platform
masks. Held Thread remained emotionally strong but carried an unavoidable C,
refresh, and loading-indicator risk.

## 7. Final geometry and color

- View box: `0 0 1024 1024`.
- Master shapes: exactly two filled paths, `span` and `forward-step`.
- Essential bounds: x 236–914, y 130–878.
- Conservatively clear of launcher corners.
- Main field: deep forest `#073E36`.
- Primary span: warm paper `#F7F1E6`.
- Forward support: amber `#E1A64A`.
- Transparent in-app mark: brand forest `#0A4F44` plus amber.
- Monochrome: both paths use one color; the gap does all identity work.

No gradient, shadow, bevel, texture, lighting, raster image, external font, or
typed letter is part of the icon identity.

## 8. Recall-test protocol

Human testing remains required:

1. Show the icon alone for five seconds.
2. Hide it and ask the participant to describe the main shape in one sentence.
3. Ask for a quick sketch without showing the icon again.
4. After at least ten minutes, show it among unrelated icons and record time to
   first correct selection.
5. Repeat among personal-CRM and mainstream-CRM competitors.
6. Ask for three emotional or personality words.
7. Do not ask the participant to infer the full feature set.

Record whether participants recall **arch + broken/forward support**, whether
they locate it in under three seconds, and whether associations cluster around
calm, thoughtful, human, or forward. Also record spontaneous "n dot," doorway,
or pin readings rather than correcting them.
